# Executive summary

## Recommendation
Send the **Mens E-Mail** campaign. It raised revenue per customer by $0.77 over no e-mail (95% CI $0.48 to $1.05), a 118% increase, versus $0.42 for the Womens E-Mail campaign.

At 30% gross margin and $0.10 per e-mail, sending it to 100,000 customers would add about $76,983 in revenue and $13,095 in gross profit. The campaign stays profitable while e-mails cost less than $0.23 each.

## Targeting
A next-best-action model that picks the most profitable option per customer averaged $-0.035 profit per customer versus e-mailing everyone with the best single campaign, and won in 5% of 20 repeated test splits. That is not a reliable gain, so the simpler blanket campaign is the better decision until a larger test shows otherwise.

## Evidence quality
- Randomization check: arm sizes match an even split (chi-square p = 0.90); largest covariate imbalance |SMD| = 0.009 (below 0.1 is balanced).
- 6 of 6 lift tests remain significant after Holm correction.
- Spend is revenue, not profit; margin and e-mail cost are assumptions varied in the sensitivity grid.
- The test ran for two weeks in 2008, so long-term effects (unsubscribes, repeat purchases) are unknown.
